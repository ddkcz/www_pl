Personal projects

Engineering is more than a job to me — it is a way of looking at everyday problems. I enjoy taking things apart, looking for solutions and, above all, learning by doing. Every project below was completely new to me. Some were large and expensive undertakings, so I began each one with research, learning and design, and finished with physical implementation.

I am not afraid of subjects I do not yet know. On the contrary, those are the challenges that interest me most. The greatest satisfaction comes when “I do not know how to do this” turns into “it works”.

If you would like to learn more about my personal projects, please get in touch. They are experiments rather than commercial projects. I am happy to share my experience and knowledge or support your own project. Need drawings? Contact me.

Deck with a pergola

The deck and pergola were designed to create a comfortable space for relaxation and social gatherings. I began without previous experience of this type of structure. I analysed available structural solutions, materials, anchoring methods and construction requirements from scratch. Based on the knowledge gathered, I prepared a 3D model of the complete structure and then built it.


The deck was built from C24 structural timber on a double layer of spruce joists. The first 45 × 120 mm layer was mounted on metal anchors embedded in concrete foundations, providing stable and durable support while allowing the required slope to be adjusted easily — a target of 2% towards drainage, regulated with M12 nuts. A second 45 × 45 mm joist layer stiffened the structure and provided suitable support for the deck boards.

I installed the boards with a concealed fastening system that also provides expansion gaps and ventilation. The tali hardwood boards offer high durability and weather resistance. The pergola was designed for both appearance and function. Its B24 glulam structure provides strength, stability and a high-quality finish, while allowing Roman blinds to be installed when time permits.


After completing the structure, I asked professional contractors to quote an equivalent deck and pergola using the same materials. The estimates ranged from PLN 50,000 to 60,000. By completing the project myself, I kept the cost to PLN 15,000, including materials, tools and installation accessories. My time was not included because this was a hobby project driven by a passion for making things. The final photographs show a few unfinished details — as with every DIY project, they will be completed eventually.

Two-storey 38 m² timber-frame building

A timber-frame building with a footprint of 38 m², designed to maximise available space while retaining an attractive and functional form. Timber-frame construction enables fast and efficient erection. The building is currently at the design stage, with construction planned for Q4 2026.

Vegetable garden with a semi-automatic irrigation system

I previously had almost no experience with gardening or irrigation. I began with research into plant requirements, installation design and solutions that could reduce daily garden maintenance. I then designed the garden and complete system, prepared 3D models, selected components and built a working solution that automates much of the watering process. It now requires only connecting the water supply and adjusting a few valves. The semi-automatic system cost PLN 300.

Modular staircase with integrated storage

The house I built has limited storage, so I wanted the staircase to serve both as access to the upper floor and as storage. I could not find a contractor willing to build the staircase structure with integrated storage; each one referred me to a cabinetmaker for an additional enclosure. Rather than paying twice, I built the stairs myself using offcuts from the timber-frame house, completing the project for PLN 700.

The staircase is fully functional, with large drawers rated to 120 kg and clever access to otherwise unused spaces. I built it as modules in my uncle's workshop and then installed them in place. I plan to add shoe drawers beneath every possible step and finish the structure with plywood. As is often the case with DIY projects, time is the greatest constraint, so the stairs currently remain unfinished with temporary OSB treads. Completion is planned for Q4 2026.
