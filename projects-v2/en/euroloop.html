Amber II 720 kW — EV charging station

Mechanical development of an EV charging station: from an industrial-design concept through prototypes and revisions to series-production launch and continued production support.

RoleSenior Mechanical Engineer / Product Lead
Context

From an industrial-design concept to a complete mechanical system

When I joined the project, the startup employed seven people, operated a laboratory of approximately 200 m², and the Amber II charger existed as an industrial-design concept.


Working with one other mechanical engineer, we completed the mechanical part of the entire charger in six months. Over the following 2.5 years, my scope covered further development of the assigned systems, revisions, problem analysis, supplier cooperation, and assembly and production support.

Challenge

Integrate high-power systems into a product ready for assembly and further development

The mechanical scope included the power module, the S-HUB intelligent power-allocation system, busbar routing through the charger, controller placement and cable routing. The greatest technical challenges were EMC and component cooling, both of which were resolved during the work.

720 kW 50 kW EMC Cooling Busbars NPI / NPD
Role and work

A scope spanning engineering and the product-development process

The responsibility combined mechanical design, prototyping and validation with production readiness, change management and coordination of module development.

01 · DESIGN

Mechanical architecture

  • Designing more than 70% of the mechanical components in the first product version.
  • Designing the 50 kW power module and S-HUB intelligent power-allocation system for the 720 kW station.
  • Power-cable and busbar routes, controller placement and the layout of other internal components.
  • Sheet-metal, machined, welded and 3D-printed parts, including components supporting assembly.
  • GD&T and tolerance-stack analyses for key mechanical assemblies.
02 · PROTOTYPE AND VALIDATION

From model to subsequent revisions

  • Leading work through the complete NPI/NPD cycle: from concept and 3D design in SolidWorks to prototyping, testing and series production.
  • Supervising prototype assembly and actively supporting the product launch.
  • Root Cause Analysis, subsequent design revisions and continuous improvement of existing solutions.
  • Work on electromagnetic compatibility and effective cooling of high-power components.
  • Implementing DFMA, Design for Reliability and FMEA in product development.
03 · PRODUCTION

Suppliers, assembly and series support

  • Supplier cooperation, qualification of new vendors and optimisation of manufacturing cost and technology.
  • Assembly stations and tools that improved production and reduced assembly time.
  • Defining and optimising manufacturing procedures and R&D processes that enabled the organisation to scale.
  • Ongoing production support, analysis of assembly issues and implementation of corrective actions.
04 · PRODUCT LEAD

Changes, documentation and information flow

  • Coordinating the assigned modules from prototype to series production.
  • Managing the development backlog and planning subsequent product iterations.
  • Designing and implementing documentation, revision and PLM systems in Notion according to management requirements.
  • Building information flow between R&D and production for engineering-change management.
